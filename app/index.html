    About Beneath

    Beneath is an explorable globe of what lies under the Earth's surface. Drag the depth slider to peel back the surface, then click or tap anywhere to read what the data says about that point.

    Turn on See beneath to make the surface translucent. Every magnitude 5 and larger earthquake since 1970 then sits at the depth where it started. Under Japan, the Andes or Tonga they form sheets that dive hundreds of kilometres into the mantle, tracing plates as they sink. White rings are earthquakes from the past 7 days.

    The live layers above the surface show about 16,000 satellites moving in real time (click one to see its orbit), aircraft near the view, and public traffic cameras in California and New York State with their latest image.

    Draw a cross-section by clicking two points. Beneath the line you get the ground profile, the magnetic and gravity readings, any plate boundaries it crosses, and every earthquake nearby plotted by depth.

    This site is educational. It is not an exploration, engineering or investment tool. Data resolution is kilometres, not metres, and some datasets are decades old.
